## Meta Description

From privacy policies to collection notices and automated decision-making disclosures -- here are the exact obligations Australian trade businesses face under the Privacy Act from July 2026, with a practical compliance checklist.

## TL;DR Draft

> **TL;DR:** Once covered by the Privacy Act, your trade business must comply with 13 Australian Privacy Principles (APPs). The big obligations: publish a compliant privacy policy (APP 1), issue collection notices when you gather customer data (APP 5), secure that data (APP 11), and -- from 10 December 2026 -- disclose any automated decision-making in your privacy policy (new APP 1.8). The OAIC has already fined businesses $62,600 per offence for non-compliant policies. Source: OAIC / Privacy Act 1988.

### H2: What Is an APP Entity and Are You One? (250--300 words)

- **Answer-first**: An APP entity is any organisation covered by the Privacy Act. From 1 July 2026, if your trade business loses the small business exemption (via Tranche 2 or future full removal), you become an APP entity.
- Cover: Definition, who is captured now vs July 2026, the "opt-in" option for businesses that want to get ahead
- **Key stat**: Over 100,000 businesses become APP entities on 1 July 2026 (OAIC)

### H2: The 13 Australian Privacy Principles -- Ranked by Relevance for Trade Businesses (400--500 words)

- **Answer-first**: Not all 13 APPs hit equally. For a carpet cleaning or restoration business, APPs 1, 3, 5, 6, 8, 11, and 13 are the ones that will require action.
- Cover: Brief description of each APP, ranked by operational impact for trades. Group into "must act now", "moderate relevance", and "low relevance for most trades"
- **Chart**: Grouped bar chart or heatmap showing relevance of each APP to trade businesses (high/medium/low)
- **Key stat**: OAIC's compliance sweep focused on face-to-face data collection businesses -- the exact profile of a carpet cleaner visiting a customer's home (OAIC, January 2026)

### H2: Your Privacy Policy -- What Must It Contain? (300--400 words)

- **Answer-first**: APP 1 requires a clearly expressed and up-to-date privacy policy. It must cover: what information you collect, why, how it's stored, who you share it with, how customers can access/correct their data, and how to complain.
- Cover: Minimum required elements, plain-English writing requirements, where to publish it (website, intake forms, quotes), OAIC's Privacy Foundations self-assessment tool
- **Image**: Screenshot mockup of a sample trade business privacy policy page
- **Key stat**: $62,600 per offence for non-compliant privacy policies (OAIC enforcement powers)

### H2: Collection Notices -- What to Say When You Gather Data (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: APP 5 requires you to notify individuals at or before the time you collect their personal information. For a carpet cleaner, that means when you take a booking, visit a home, or collect payment details.
- Cover: When a collection notice is needed, what it must contain, practical examples (booking form, on-site job sheet, online quote request)
- **Image**: Pixabay -- clipboard or tablet with form, trade setting
- **Key stat**: 37% of data breaches in H1 2025 were caused by human error (OAIC NDB Report) -- proper collection practices reduce this risk

### H2: Data Security -- APP 11 Requirements (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: You must take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference, loss, and unauthorised access. "Reasonable" scales with your business size and data sensitivity.
- Cover: What "reasonable steps" means for a small trade business, digital security (password policies, encrypted storage), physical security (job sheets, paper records), staff training
- **Chart**: Donut chart showing breach sources (59% malicious attacks, 37% human error, 4% system faults -- OAIC H1 2025)
- **Key stat**: Average of 10,000 individuals affected per cyber incident (OAIC NDB Report H1 2025)

### H2: Automated Decision-Making -- The December 2026 Deadline (300--400 words)

- **Answer-first**: From 10 December 2026, if you use any computer program to make or substantially assist in making decisions about individuals, you must disclose this in your privacy policy under new APP 1.8.
- Cover: What counts as ADM (automated quoting, CRM-based customer scoring, AI-powered scheduling), what you must disclose (kinds of personal info used, kinds of decisions made solely by software, kinds of decisions substantially assisted by software), practical examples for trade businesses
- **Image**: Pixabay -- laptop with data dashboard, trade business context
- **Key stat**: New APP 1.8 commences 10 December 2026 (Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024)

### H2: Compliance Checklist for Trade Businesses (200--250 words)

- **Answer-first**: Summary checklist in a numbered/checkbox format:
  1. Map all personal information your business collects
  2. Draft or update your privacy policy (use OAIC template)
  3. Create collection notices for each data touchpoint
  4. Review data security measures (digital + physical)
  5. Train staff on privacy obligations
  6. Audit automated decision-making systems (CRM, quoting tools)
  7. Set up a data breach response plan
  8. Register with AUSTRAC if in a Tranche 2 sector
- **Chart**: Progress/checklist visual or step chart

### FAQ Section (5 items)

1. **Can I use a template privacy policy?** -- Yes, the OAIC provides a Privacy Foundations self-assessment tool and guidance on writing privacy policies. Customise it to your specific data practices. Source: OAIC.
2. **Do I need to notify customers if I use software to generate quotes?** -- From 10 December 2026, if your quoting software uses personal information to make or assist decisions, you must disclose this in your privacy policy. Source: Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024.
3. **What if I only collect names and phone numbers?** -- Even minimal personal information is covered. You still need a privacy policy and collection notices. Source: APP 1 and APP 5.
4. **Do subcontractors need their own privacy policies?** -- If a subcontractor collects personal information independently (not just as your agent), they likely need their own compliance. Source: OAIC guidance.
5. **How do I handle data from before July 2026?** -- Personal information collected before you became an APP entity is still subject to the APPs once you are covered. You should audit existing records. Source: OAIC transition guidance.
