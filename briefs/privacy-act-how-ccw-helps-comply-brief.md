### H2: What Is Privacy-by-Design and Why Does It Matter? (250--300 words)

- **Answer-first**: Privacy-by-design means building privacy protections into your systems and processes from the start, rather than retrofitting them. The OAIC explicitly recommends this approach.
- Cover: The 7 foundational principles of privacy-by-design (summarised), why it is cheaper and more effective than bolt-on compliance, how trade businesses can apply it
- **Key stat**: 37% of data breaches are caused by human error (OAIC H1 2025) -- privacy-by-design reduces human error by automating protections

### H2: Step 1 -- Audit Your Data Flows (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: Before you can comply, you need to know what data you collect, where it goes, and who has access. For a carpet cleaning business, this includes customer names, addresses, phone numbers, job site photos, payment details, and subcontractor information.
- Cover: How to map data touchpoints (booking form, on-site job sheet, invoicing, CRM, email), template for a data flow map, what to look for
- **Image**: Pixabay -- whiteboard or flowchart diagram
- **Key stat**: CCW identified 14 data touchpoints in its own ERP audit (original data)

### H2: Step 2 -- Write Your Privacy Policy (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: Use the OAIC's guidance as a starting point, then customise for your trade business. Your policy must cover: what you collect, why, how it is stored, who sees it, and how customers can access or correct their data.
- Cover: OAIC template resources, plain-English writing tips, where to publish (website footer, booking confirmation emails, on-site paperwork), common mistakes to avoid
- **Image**: Screenshot or mockup of a sample trade business privacy policy
- **Key stat**: $62,600 per offence for non-compliant privacy policies (OAIC)

### H2: Step 3 -- Add Collection Notices to Every Touchpoint (250--300 words)

- **Answer-first**: Every time you collect personal information, you must tell the individual what you are collecting and why. For trade businesses, this means notices on booking forms, job sheets, quote requests, and payment pages.
- Cover: Examples of collection notices for each touchpoint, one-line vs full notice, how CCW's ERP can auto-include collection notices on customer-facing forms
- **Chart**: Flow chart showing data collection touchpoints for a typical trade business (booking -> site visit -> quote -> job -> invoice -> follow-up)

### H2: Step 4 -- Secure Your Systems and Train Your Team (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: APP 11 requires "reasonable steps" to protect data. For a small trade business, reasonable steps include strong passwords, encrypted storage, limited access, and staff training.
- Cover: Digital security checklist (MFA, encrypted databases, regular backups), physical security (locking filing cabinets, shredding old job sheets), staff training requirements, how CCW's ERP handles security (role-based access, audit logs, encrypted storage)
- **Image**: Pixabay -- padlock or security concept, trade/business setting
- **Key stat**: 59% of breaches from malicious attacks, 37% from human error (OAIC H1 2025) -- training addresses the 37%

### H2: Step 5 -- Prepare for Automated Decision-Making Rules (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: If your business uses software to generate quotes, schedule jobs, score customers, or make any decision that affects individuals, you must disclose this in your privacy policy by 10 December 2026.
- Cover: What counts as ADM in a trade business (CRM scoring, automated scheduling, AI-powered recommendations, dynamic pricing), what to disclose, how CCW's ERP is preparing (transparent algorithms, ADM disclosure documentation, customer opt-out mechanisms)
- **Image**: CCW ERP screenshot showing quote generation or scheduling feature
- **Key stat**: New APP 1.8 requires disclosure of ADM from 10 December 2026 (Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024)

### H2: How CCW's ERP Builds Privacy Into Your Workflow (300--400 words)

- **Answer-first**: CCW's ERP is designed with privacy-by-design principles. Rather than bolting compliance onto your workflow, it builds it in.
- Cover: Feature-by-feature walkthrough:
  - **Role-based access control**: Limit who can see customer data
  - **Audit logs**: Track every data access and modification
  - **Consent tracking**: Record when and how customers consented to data collection
  - **Data retention controls**: Automatically flag or archive data past retention periods
  - **Collection notice integration**: Auto-include privacy notices on quotes, invoices, and booking confirmations
  - **Export and deletion**: Tools for responding to customer data access or deletion requests
  - **ADM transparency**: Documentation of which features use automated decision-making
- **Chart**: Comparison table -- manual compliance vs CCW ERP-assisted compliance (time/effort)
- **Key stat**: Privacy-by-design reduces compliance effort and breach risk simultaneously

### FAQ Section (4 items)

1. **How much does Privacy Act compliance cost a small trade business?** -- The OAIC provides free guidance and templates. The main costs are time (policy drafting, staff training) and potentially legal review. Using an ERP with built-in privacy features significantly reduces ongoing compliance effort. Source: OAIC.
2. **Can CCW's ERP handle data deletion requests?** -- Yes. The ERP includes tools for locating, exporting, and deleting customer data in response to access or correction requests under APP 12 and APP 13. Source: CCW product roadmap.
3. **Do I need to hire a privacy officer?** -- Not necessarily. Small trade businesses can designate an existing team member as the privacy contact. The key is that someone is responsible for handling privacy inquiries and breaches. Source: OAIC guidance.
4. **What if I already use ServiceTitan or simPRO?** -- These platforms handle data processing but you remain the data controller. You still need your own privacy policy, collection notices, and compliance processes regardless of which software you use. CCW's ERP is the only AU-built option with Privacy Act-specific compliance features. Source: OAIC data controller guidance.
