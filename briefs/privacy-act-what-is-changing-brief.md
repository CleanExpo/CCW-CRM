## TL;DR Draft

> **TL;DR:** Australia is removing the $3 million small business Privacy Act exemption. From 1 July 2026, over 100,000 businesses -- including trade operators handling customer data for cleaning, restoration, and equipment sales -- must comply with the Australian Privacy Principles for the first time. Penalties reach $50 million or 30% of turnover. Three key dates: 31 March 2026 (AUSTRAC enrolment opens), 1 July 2026 (AML/CTF Tranche 2 obligations start), and 10 December 2026 (automated decision-making transparency rules commence). Source: OAIC / Attorney-General's Department.

### H2: What Is the Small Business Privacy Exemption? (250--300 words)

- **Answer-first**: Since 2000, businesses with under $3M annual turnover have been exempt from the Privacy Act 1988. That covers roughly 97.3% of Australia's 2.73 million businesses (ABS, June 2025).
- Cover: History of the exemption, why it existed, who it covered
- **Image**: Pixabay -- small Australian trade van / workshop
- **Key stat**: 97.3% of Australian businesses are small businesses (ABS, June 2025)

### H2: Why Is the Exemption Being Removed? (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: The Attorney-General's Privacy Act Review recommended removal because the digital economy means even small businesses now handle significant personal data
- Cover: AML/CTF Tranche 2 connection, government agreed-in-principle to full removal, phased approach starting with high-risk sectors
- **Chart**: Timeline bar chart showing key dates (March 2026, July 2026, December 2026)
- **Key stat**: 100,000+ businesses affected in the first wave (Helios Salinger, March 2026)

### H2: What Is AML/CTF Tranche 2 and How Does It Connect? (300--400 words)

- **Answer-first**: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act reform ("Tranche 2") extends AML/CTF obligations to new sectors from 1 July 2026. Critically, businesses caught by Tranche 2 also lose their Privacy Act small business exemption.
- Cover: Which sectors are affected (lawyers, accountants, real estate, dealers in high-value goods), AUSTRAC enrolment, customer due diligence
- **Key stat**: AML/CTF obligations commence for Tranche 2 entities on 1 July 2026, with enrolment by 29 July 2026 (AUSTRAC)

### H2: Three Deadlines Every Trade Business Must Know (300--350 words)

- **Answer-first**: Open with the three dates in a summary table
- Cover: 31 March 2026 (AUSTRAC enrolment opens for Tranche 2), 1 July 2026 (Tranche 2 obligations commence + Privacy Act exemption lost), 10 December 2026 (automated decision-making transparency rules)
- **Chart**: Horizontal timeline or Gantt-style bar chart with the 3 deadlines
- **Key stat**: $62,600 per offence for non-compliant privacy policies; up to $50M or 30% turnover for serious breaches (OAIC)

### H2: Does This Affect Carpet Cleaners, Restorers, and Equipment Suppliers? (300--400 words)

- **Answer-first**: Yes. Even if your business is not directly in a Tranche 2 sector, the government has agreed in principle to remove the exemption entirely. Plus, any trade business collecting customer data through digital systems (CRMs, online ordering, payment processing) should prepare now.
- Cover: Types of data trade businesses collect (names, addresses, job site details, payment info, photos of property), why "we're just cleaners" is not a defence, the OAIC compliance sweep targeting face-to-face data collection businesses
- **Image**: Pixabay -- carpet cleaner at work in a residential setting
- **Key stat**: OAIC's first compliance sweep reviewed 60 businesses in high-risk face-to-face data collection sectors (National Law Review, January 2026)

### H2: What Are the Penalties for Non-Compliance? (250--300 words)

- **Answer-first**: Penalties are severe -- up to $50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover (whichever is greater) for corporations
- Cover: Tiered penalty structure, $62,600 per-offence penalties for privacy policy failures, the new statutory tort allowing individuals to sue directly (commenced June 2025)
- **Chart**: Donut or comparison chart showing penalty tiers
- **Key stat**: Individuals can face penalties up to $2.5M; corporations up to $50M (Privacy Act)

### FAQ Section (5 items)

1. **Does the Privacy Act apply to sole traders?** -- Yes, once the exemption is removed, sole traders handling personal information must comply with the APPs regardless of turnover. Source: OAIC.
2. **When exactly does the small business exemption end?** -- For Tranche 2 sectors, 1 July 2026. For all remaining small businesses, the government has agreed in principle but has not set a final date. Source: Attorney-General's Department.
3. **What is an APP entity?** -- An "Australian Privacy Principle entity" is any organisation or agency covered by the Privacy Act. From July 2026, this will include newly captured Tranche 2 businesses. Source: OAIC.
4. **Do I need a privacy policy even if I only clean carpets?** -- If you collect personal information (customer names, addresses, photos, payment details), yes. The OAIC is actively sweeping businesses in face-to-face data collection sectors. Source: OAIC compliance sweep, January 2026.
5. **What is the automated decision-making rule starting December 2026?** -- From 10 December 2026, any APP entity using computer programs to make or assist decisions affecting individuals must disclose this in their privacy policy. Source: Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024.
