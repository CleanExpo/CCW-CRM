## H1: Mould Remediation Equipment: The Complete Guide for Australian Contractors (2026)

**Intro**

Mould remediation is one of the fastest-growing specialisations in the Australian restoration industry. With increasing awareness of indoor air quality — and tighter building codes following La Niña flooding events — demand for qualified mould remediators is outpacing supply.

But getting the work isn't the hard part. Doing it safely, thoroughly, and to IICRC S520 standard requires the right equipment. This guide covers every category of gear you need: detection, containment, air filtration, removal, drying, and PPE.

---

## Section 1: Understanding the Remediation Process

Before buying equipment, understand the workflow. IICRC S520 (Standard and Reference Guide for Professional Mould Remediation) defines a structured process:

1. **Assessment** — Moisture mapping, visual inspection, air sampling
2. **Containment** — Isolate affected areas to prevent spore spread
3. **Filtration** — Establish negative air pressure using HEPA-filtered machines
4. **Removal** — Physical removal of contaminated materials
5. **Cleaning** — HEPA vacuuming, antimicrobial treatment, HEPA wiping
6. **Drying** — Structural drying to prevent recurrence
7. **Verification** — Post-remediation testing to confirm clearance

Each stage requires specific equipment. Shortcuts in any stage compromise both the outcome and your liability.

## Section 2: Moisture Detection Equipment

You cannot remediate what you cannot find. Moisture detection is the foundation of every mould job.

### Moisture Meters

**Penetrating meters** (pin-type) measure conductivity between two probes inserted into the material. Best for timber framing, skirting boards, and subfloors.

**Non-penetrating meters** (pad/scan type) use radio frequency or capacitance to detect moisture without surface damage. Essential for finished walls and flooring where penetration isn't acceptable.

**What to buy:** A quality dual-mode meter (both pin and scan) from Protimeter, Flir, or similar. Budget $300–$800 AUD.

### Thermal Imaging Cameras

Thermal cameras (infrared) detect temperature differentials that indicate moisture behind walls and under floors. A wet wall section is typically cooler than dry surroundings.

Not a substitute for a moisture meter — thermal cameras show anomalies that require confirmation with a meter. Used together, they dramatically speed up the assessment phase.

**Budget**: $1,200–$4,500 AUD for a suitable handheld unit.

### Hygrometers / Data Loggers

Relative humidity in the remediation zone and adjacent areas needs continuous monitoring. Data loggers record conditions over time — useful for proving drying progression to clients and insurers.

## Section 3: Containment Equipment

Containment prevents cross-contamination to unaffected areas. Without containment, you're just moving spores around.

### Poly Sheeting and Tape

Heavy-duty (6-mil minimum) polyethylene sheeting to seal doorways, HVAC vents, and create physical barriers. Use negative-pressure tape that bonds to drywall, timber, and glass.

**Setup**: Double-layer doorway flaps (airlock entry) for high-contamination zones.

### Negative Air Machines (Air Scrubbers)

The cornerstone of containment. A negative air machine:

- Creates lower pressure inside the remediation zone than the surrounding rooms
- Forces air to flow inward (toward the machine) rather than outward
- Exhausts filtered air outside the building (or into HEPA-filtered return air)

**Spec minimum for mould work**: HEPA-filtered, at least 4-stage filtration, minimum 500 CFM capacity. For larger areas, calculate 6–10 air changes per hour.

## Section 4: HEPA Filtration Equipment

HEPA (High-Efficiency Particulate Air) filtration is mandatory for mould work. Standard vacuum cleaners recirculate spores — HEPA captures them.

### HEPA Vacuum Cleaners

HEPA-rated industrial vacuums with fine-particle certification (H13 or H14 class) for:

- Vacuuming loose spores from surfaces prior to wiping
- Cleaning up remediation debris
- Vacuuming inside wall cavities before treatment

**Never use a standard vacuum on mould.** It exhausts spores through the motor and bag into the air.

### Air Scrubbers vs. Negative Air Machines

Technically different applications:

- **Air scrubber**: Recirculates air through HEPA filters within the space (improving air quality)
- **Negative air machine**: Exhausts filtered air outside (maintaining pressure differential)

Both use HEPA filtration. For active mould remediation, negative air machines providing exhaust are required. Air scrubbers support post-remediation air quality improvement.

## Section 5: Antimicrobial Treatment Equipment

After physical removal, surfaces require antimicrobial treatment to kill remaining spores and prevent regrowth.

### Electrostatic Sprayers

Electrostatic sprayers apply antimicrobial chemical as a charged mist that wraps around objects — covering all surfaces (top, bottom, sides) with a single pass. Far more effective than trigger sprayers for treating wall cavities, framing, and irregular surfaces.

### HEPA Foggers

ULV (Ultra Low Volume) foggers distribute antimicrobial solution as a fine aerosol throughout the space. Used for post-remediation treatment of large areas and HVAC systems.

**Important**: Foggers are not a substitute for physical removal and cleaning. Fogging over active mould growth is not compliant with S520.

## Section 6: Drying Equipment

After remediation, the affected structure must be dried to prevent recurrence. Mould requires moisture above 60% relative humidity to grow.

### Dehumidifiers

Commercial dehumidifiers (LGR — Low Grain Refrigerant type) are the gold standard for structural drying. LGR units are effective at lower temperature/humidity conditions that standard dehumidifiers struggle with.

**Placement**: 1 dehumidifier per 40–50m² in affected zones. For contained remediation areas, one high-capacity unit may be sufficient.

### Air Movers / Axial Fans

High-volume air movers direct airflow across wet surfaces to accelerate evaporation into the air, which the dehumidifier then captures. The combination of air mover + dehumidifier is far more effective than either alone.

**Ratio**: Typically 3–5 air movers per dehumidifier, though this varies by materials and conditions.

## Section 7: Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Working with mould is a health hazard. Correct PPE is non-negotiable.

### Respiratory Protection

- **N95 / P2 masks**: Minimum for light mould work and supervision
- **Half-face respirator with P100 / N100 cartridges**: Required for active remediation
- **Full-face respirator**: For high-contamination areas or where eye protection is needed alongside filtration

### Body Protection

- **Disposable Tyvek suits** (Type 5/6): Full-body coverage to prevent dermal and clothing contamination
- **Gloves**: Nitrile, minimum 5-mil thickness, gauntlet length preferred
- **Boot covers or dedicated work boots**: Prevent tracking spores to clean areas

### Decontamination

Establish a decon area at the containment entrance. Workers remove and bag Tyvek suits inside the containment zone before exiting. HEPA vacuuming of boots and equipment prevents cross-contamination.

## Section 8: Documentation and Reporting Tools

Professional mould remediators document every step. This protects you from liability and supports insurance claims.

- **Air sampling pumps and cassettes**: Pre- and post-remediation air samples sent to NATA-accredited labs
- **Moisture documentation software**: Apps like DryBook or Encircle for photo documentation and moisture readings
- **Thermal imaging reports**: Exported from camera software with annotated findings

## FAQ Section (FAQPage JSON-LD)

**Q1: Do I need special certification for mould remediation in Australia?**
While there is no single mandatory licence for mould remediation in Australia, the industry standard is the IICRC Applied Microbial Remediation Technician (AMRT) certification. It demonstrates competency in S520 protocols and is increasingly required by insurance companies and property managers.

**Q2: What's the difference between an air scrubber and a negative air machine?**
An air scrubber recirculates air within the space through HEPA filters, improving air quality. A negative air machine exhausts HEPA-filtered air outside the building, creating negative pressure that prevents spore migration. Both use HEPA filtration, but negative air machines are required for active remediation containment.

**Q3: How do I know my HEPA filter is actually working?**
Genuine HEPA-class filters (H13 or H14) are certified to capture 99.95–99.995% of particles 0.3 microns or larger. Purchase from reputable suppliers and replace filters on the manufacturer's schedule. A damaged or improperly seated HEPA filter provides no protection.

**Q4: Can I use household bleach for mould remediation?**
No. Household bleach (sodium hypochlorite) is not recommended for structural mould remediation. It doesn't penetrate porous materials, and the water content can actually increase moisture in the substrate. IICRC S520 recommends EPA-registered antimicrobials formulated for remediation work.

**Q5: How much equipment do I need to start a mould remediation service?**
A basic mobilised kit for single-room jobs includes: moisture meter, HEPA vacuum, 1 negative air machine, 1 dehumidifier, 3 air movers, containment materials, electrostatic sprayer, and PPE. Budget $8,000–$15,000 AUD for a professional entry-level setup.
