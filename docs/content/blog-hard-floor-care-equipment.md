## H1: Hard Floor Care Equipment: The Professional Guide for Australian Cleaners (2026)

**Intro**

Hard floor cleaning is the fastest-growing add-on service for Australian carpet cleaning professionals. Tile, grout, stone, timber, vinyl plank — every property has hard floors, and most of them haven't been professionally cleaned in years.

The right equipment transforms hard floor work from labour-intensive scrubbing into a fast, high-margin service. This guide covers the key equipment categories, the floor types each tool handles, and how to build a profitable hard floor service from your existing van.

---

## Section 1: Hard Floor Types and Their Requirements

Before buying equipment, understand the floor surfaces you'll be working on:

| Floor Type                                    | Challenges                 | Key Equipment                              |
| --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Ceramic/porcelain tile                        | Grout line soiling, scale  | Rotary spinner, grout brush                |
| Natural stone (travertine, marble, limestone) | Etching risk, sealing      | Stone-safe chemistry, polishing pads       |
| Engineered timber                             | Water sensitivity          | Low-moisture methods, microfibre           |
| Solid hardwood                                | Scratch risk, water damage | Hardwood-specific chemistry, floor machine |
| Vinyl plank (LVP/LVT)                         | No harsh chemicals         | Neutral pH cleaner, soft pad               |
| Concrete                                      | Heavy soiling, sealing     | Rotary machine, diamond grind pads         |
| Slate                                         | Uneven surface, sealing    | Spinner, grout brush, penetrating sealer   |

Never use a one-size-fits-all approach. Alkaline tile and grout chemistry that works well on ceramics will etch and damage marble or travertine. Chemistry selection is as important as equipment selection.

## Section 2: Rotary Spinners / Tile and Grout Cleaners

The single most effective hard floor tool for tile and grout is a rotary spinning head that connects to a truckmount or portable extractor.

### How It Works

A rotary tile cleaning tool (also called a spinner or tile cleaning head) connects to the truckmount's solution and vacuum lines. Inside the sealed housing, water jets rotate under high pressure, blasting soil from tile surfaces and grout lines. Dirty water is immediately extracted back through the machine.

**Key advantages over scrubbing:**

- 5–10x faster than brush scrubbing
- No transfer of soiled water to adjacent areas
- Consistent results regardless of operator fatigue
- Works with the truckmount you already own

### Types

**Enclosed spinners (round)**: Standard for residential kitchens, bathrooms, and laundries. Typically 280–450mm diameter. Best for open tile areas.

**Jet spinners (rectangular/elongated)**: Designed for narrow grout lines, tiled walls, and areas where a circular head won't fit.

**Walk-behind units**: For commercial areas (shopping centres, restaurant kitchens). Larger head diameter, faster area coverage.

### Specifications to Compare

- **Connection**: Check your truckmount's solution pressure and that the spinner's pressure rating matches
- **Head size**: Larger heads cover more area; smaller heads offer more control in tight spaces
- **Material**: Stainless steel construction for durability

## Section 3: Rotary Floor Machines

A rotary floor machine (single-disc scrubber) is the workhorse of hard floor maintenance. Used for:

- Pre-agitation before extraction on tile
- Stripping old floor finish (wax, sealer)
- Applying floor finish and sealer
- Buffing and polishing
- Crystallisation of marble and limestone

### Speeds and Applications

| Speed (RPM)                             | Application                           |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 175 RPM                                 | Stripping, scrubbing, heavy soiling   |
| 300–400 RPM                             | General scrubbing, pad cleaning       |
| 1,000–1,500 RPM (high-speed burnisher)  | Buff and shine maintenance            |
| 3,000+ RPM (ultra-high speed burnisher) | High-gloss finish on commercial vinyl |

Most operators start with a 175 RPM machine and add a high-speed burnisher once they've built a commercial client base.

### Pads and Discs

The pad determines what you're doing:

- **Black or brown pad**: Stripping (most aggressive)
- **Red pad**: Light scrubbing and cleaning
- **White or tan pad**: Buffing and polishing
- **Natural fibre / horsehair**: Stone care and crystallisation
- **Diamond grind pads**: Concrete grinding and honing (set up separately)

## Section 4: Stone Restoration Equipment

Natural stone (marble, travertine, limestone, terrazzo) requires specialised equipment and training. Stone is permanently damaged by wrong chemistry — a single acid spill can etch the surface.

### Stone Polishing System

Diamond polishing pads in progressive grits (50, 100, 200, 400, 800, 1500, 3000) used with a rotary machine restore stone to a factory finish. Process:

1. Grind away scratches and staining with low-grit diamond pads (50–200)
2. Hone to refine the surface (400–800)
3. Polish to restore gloss (1500–3000)
4. Apply sealer for protection

### Stone Crystallisation

A chemical process (using oxalic acid and steel wool or crystallisation pads) that creates a hard, glossy surface on marble and limestone without grinding. Faster than diamond polishing for maintenance work.

### Chemistry Rules for Stone

- **Never** use acidic cleaners on marble, travertine, or limestone (etches permanently)
- **Never** use alkaline strippers at full strength on polished stone
- Use pH-neutral stone cleaners for maintenance
- Seal all natural stone after restoration — penetrating sealers for porous stone, topical sealers for polished marble

## Section 5: Floor Cleaning and Maintenance Machines

For ongoing maintenance contracts, particularly in commercial settings, consider:

### Auto Scrubbers

Self-propelled machines that scrub and collect dirty water simultaneously. Walk-behind units for small-medium areas; ride-on units for warehouses and large retail.

For a carpet cleaning business adding hard floor maintenance contracts, a small walk-behind auto scrubber ($3,500–$7,000 AUD) enables competitive quoting on commercial floor maintenance.

### Steam Cleaners (Vapour Steamers)

Commercial vapour steamers for sanitisation and chemical-free cleaning of tile, grout, and sealed stone. Popular in hospitality and food service where chemical residue is a concern.

### Microfibre Flat Mopping Systems

For ongoing maintenance between machine-cleaned sessions. Commercial microfibre flat mop systems are faster and more hygienic than traditional string mops.

## Section 6: Chemistry for Hard Floors

Selecting the right chemistry is as important as the equipment:

| Floor Type             | Cleaner Type                  | pH Range |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------- | -------- |
| Ceramic/porcelain tile | Alkaline tile & grout cleaner | 9–12     |
| Natural stone          | Neutral stone cleaner         | 6–8      |
| Sealed hardwood        | Hardwood floor cleaner        | 7–8      |
| Vinyl/LVP              | Neutral all-purpose           | 7        |
| Concrete               | Degreaser or alkaline         | 9–12     |
| Polished marble        | pH-neutral only               | 7        |

**Grout sealing chemistry**: After cleaning tile, apply a penetrating grout sealer to protect against future staining. It's a natural upsell on every tile cleaning job.

## FAQ Section (FAQPage JSON-LD)

**Q1: Can I use my truckmount to clean tile and grout?**
Yes — with a rotary tile cleaning attachment (spinner head). The truckmount provides the pressure and extraction needed for effective tile and grout cleaning. Most truckmount operators use a rotary spinner to add tile cleaning as an upsell on carpet jobs.

**Q2: How much can I charge for tile and grout cleaning in Australia?**
Tile and grout cleaning typically prices at $4–$8 per square metre for residential work, or $50–$120 per room for bathroom and kitchen jobs. Commercial tile cleaning in restaurants and shopping centres can command $8–$15 per square metre.

**Q3: What's the difference between crystallisation and diamond polishing for marble?**
Diamond polishing uses abrasive diamond pads to physically remove the damaged surface layer and re-polish to the desired finish. Crystallisation is a chemical process that creates a hardened gloss surface using oxalic acid chemistry. Diamond polishing is more thorough for heavily scratched or etched stone; crystallisation is faster for maintenance of lightly dulled surfaces.

**Q4: Is hard floor care more profitable than carpet cleaning?**
The margin structure is different. Hard floor work typically involves more equipment cost and longer job times, but the average ticket value is higher — especially for stone restoration and commercial floor maintenance contracts. Operators who combine carpet and hard floor services achieve better revenue per vehicle.

**Q5: Do I need special training to work with marble and natural stone?**
Yes. Natural stone is easily and permanently damaged by wrong chemistry or abrasive tools. The IICRC HFT certification and/or specialised stone training is strongly recommended before offering stone restoration.
