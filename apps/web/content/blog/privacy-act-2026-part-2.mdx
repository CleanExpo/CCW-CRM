# Is Your ERP Making Automated Decisions? A Checklist for Trade Suppliers Before December 2026

Most equipment and trade suppliers know their ERP does more than store records. It approves credit, generates quotes, triggers reorders, routes jobs, and — increasingly — uses AI to do all of this faster and at scale.

From **10 December 2026**, if your system is making or significantly influencing decisions about customers or employees using their personal information, the *Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024* (POLA Act) requires you to say so in your Privacy Policy. Clearly. Specifically.

This article gives you a practical audit checklist to identify which parts of your ERP trigger the new disclosure obligation — and exactly what to document for each one.

---

## The One-Sentence Test

Before running through the checklist, apply this test to every automated feature in your stack:

> *Does this system process personal information and produce an output that affects someone's credit, pricing, service level, employment, or access to goods — with little or no genuine human review before the output takes effect?*

If yes: it's likely automated decision-making (ADM) under the new APP 1.8. You need to disclose it.

If a human reviews every output and frequently overrides it before it takes effect: it may not qualify. Document that review process — it's your evidence that the system is a recommendation tool, not a decision-maker.

---

## Part A: Credit and Accounts

### ✅ Checklist

- [ ] **Automatic credit limit assignment** — Does your ERP set or adjust a new trade account's credit limit based on a formula or algorithm (e.g., trading history, payment score, ABN age)?
- [ ] **Credit hold triggers** — Does your system automatically place an account on hold when an invoice is overdue by X days, without a human reviewing that specific account first?
- [ ] **Account approval / rejection** — Does your new account onboarding workflow auto-approve or auto-reject trade account applications?
- [ ] **Payment terms allocation** — Does the system automatically assign 7-day, 14-day, or 30-day terms based on customer data without sales review?

### What to Document

For each yes above:
- What personal information is used (e.g., name, ABN, payment history, order volume)?
- What is the decision or output (e.g., "credit limit set to $5,000")?
- Can the customer request a human review? If so, how (email to accounts team, call to branch)?
- Who owns this process — who do you contact internally if a customer disputes the outcome?

---

## Part B: Pricing and Quoting

### ✅ Checklist

- [ ] **Dynamic pricing** — Does your quoting engine or ERP automatically apply different prices to different customers based on their tier, order history, or segment?
- [ ] **AI-assisted quotes** — Does your system use AI to recommend a quote price or margin based on historical data, with the sales rep accepting the suggestion without modification most of the time?
- [ ] **Discount auto-approval** — Does your ERP automatically approve discounts below a certain threshold without a manager reviewing the specific quote?
- [ ] **Volume break calculations** — Are volume pricing tiers applied automatically at invoice time based on order quantities?

### What to Document

Dynamic pricing and AI-assisted quoting are the most likely triggers here. Key questions:

- How often do your sales reps materially modify the AI-suggested price? If the answer is "rarely," the AI is effectively making the decision.
- Do different customer segments receive structurally different pricing from the same system? Customers receiving consistently lower prices due to algorithm-assigned tier classifications is ADM.
- Document the personal data driving the segmentation (e.g., industry type, postcode, annual spend).

---

## Part C: Job Scheduling and Service Routing

### ✅ Checklist

- [ ] **Auto-assignment of technicians/contractors** — Does your job management or field service module automatically assign jobs to technicians based on location, skill, or availability algorithms?
- [ ] **Priority queuing** — Does the system automatically queue jobs differently based on customer tier (e.g., contract customers get faster scheduling)?
- [ ] **SLA breach escalation** — Does your system automatically escalate a job or notify a manager without a human making that escalation decision?
- [ ] **Route optimisation** — Does your dispatch system automatically set the order and timing of technician routes, effectively determining when a customer receives service?

### What to Document

For trade suppliers with a service division, job scheduling ADM is frequently overlooked. A customer who is a "standard" account receiving a 5-day response time while a "priority" account receives same-day — driven by an algorithm applied to their account classification — is being affected by an automated decision.

Document: the classification criteria, the personal information used (customer type, location, historical spend), and the service-level differential that results.

---

## Part D: Inventory and Order Fulfilment

### ✅ Checklist

- [ ] **Automatic backorder handling** — Does your ERP automatically push orders to backorder status and set an estimated delivery date without staff reviewing each order?
- [ ] **Allocation during stock shortage** — Does your system automatically allocate scarce stock to certain customer orders over others (e.g., higher-value customers get priority)?
- [ ] **Substitution logic** — Does your ERP automatically substitute an out-of-stock item with an equivalent, notifying the customer of the change without human review?
- [ ] **Drop-shipping triggers** — Does your system automatically route certain orders to a supplier for direct delivery based on rules, without staff selection?

### What to Document

Allocation logic during stock shortages is the key risk here — if a customer misses a project deadline because your algorithm deprioritised their order in favour of a higher-volume account, that is an ADM affecting their interests. Document the allocation rules and the personal/account data that drives them.

---

## Part E: Human Resources and Staff Management

If your business uses the same ERP (or a connected HR module) for employee management, ADM obligations extend to staff as well.

### ✅ Checklist

- [ ] **Auto-approval of leave requests** — Does your HR system automatically approve leave that doesn't conflict with coverage rules, without a manager reviewing the individual request?
- [ ] **Performance scoring** — Does your system generate automated performance scores or KPI dashboards that feed into review processes?
- [ ] **Rostering / shift allocation** — Does your workforce management system automatically generate rosters based on availability and rules, with managers typically accepting the output?
- [ ] **Payroll exception flags** — Does your system automatically flag timesheets for underpayment or overpayment review?

### What to Document

ADM in employment contexts carries heightened sensitivity — employment decisions are explicitly recognised in the POLA Act as contexts where the "significant effect" threshold is more readily met. Document leave auto-approval logic carefully, particularly if employees have limited ability to request a human review.

---

## Part F: AI Assistants and Chat Tools

### ✅ Checklist

- [ ] **AI customer service / chat** — Does your website or ERP include an AI assistant that gives customers information about their account, order status, or pricing?
- [ ] **AI email responses** — Does your system draft or send automated email responses to customer enquiries with little human review before sending?
- [ ] **AI-generated quotes or recommendations** — Does an AI tool in your sales workflow generate product recommendations or configurations that customers act on directly?

### What to Document

Customer-facing AI tools are the fastest-growing category of ADM in the trade sector. If your AI assistant tells a customer their credit application was unsuccessful, or quotes a price based on their account profile, that is ADM.

Key distinction: an AI that retrieves and displays information (e.g., "your order #1234 ships Friday") is not ADM — it is information retrieval. An AI that makes a classification, assessment, or recommendation affecting the customer's interests is ADM.

## Pulling It Together: What Goes in Your Privacy Policy

Once you've completed the audit, your Privacy Policy needs a new section along these lines (template language — have your legal adviser review before publishing):

---

**Automated Decision-Making**

We use automated decision-making systems to assist with the following processes:

*[List each ADM process you identified, e.g.:]*

- *Credit limit assessment: When you apply for a trade account, our system uses your trading history, payment behaviour, and account tenure to calculate a recommended credit limit. This process uses your name, ABN, account history, and payment record.*

- *Job scheduling prioritisation: Our job management system automatically assigns scheduling priority based on your account type and service agreement. This affects your estimated response time for service requests.*

If you would like a human to review a decision made about you by one of these systems, please contact us at [accounts@yourcompany.com.au] or call [phone number]. We will review your request within [X business days].

---

This language covers the four required elements of APP 1.8: that ADM is used, what personal information is involved, how it affects individuals, and how to request human review.

## One More Thing: Document Your Review Process

If after completing the checklist you conclude that some of your automated tools do *not* trigger APP 1.8 — because a human genuinely reviews and frequently overrides the output — **document that conclusion now**.

The OAIC may ask, in the event of a complaint, why a particular tool was not disclosed. "We considered it and concluded the human review was substantive" is a defensible position. "We didn't think about it" is not.

A simple internal memo — dated, signed off by a director — noting your audit findings and reasoning is sufficient. Store it with your compliance records.
